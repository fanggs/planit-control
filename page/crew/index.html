HOW PLANIT WORKS

Know the risks.
Know what to do.

PlanIT brings environmental conditions, on-site observations, and a next-day risk outlook into one picture for each job site.

About this preview Plant locations are real; conditions and scores are sample data. The model design below follows our scoring plan, but this preview does not run trained models. Live alerts, team-wide report sharing, and briefing delivery require connected services.

  1. 01

    Check the conditions at your site.

    Select a real power plant to review weather, extreme heat, lightning, flooding, wildfire, soil saturation, and wildlife. Read each source and timestamp; missing readings show “no data.”

  2. 02

    Act on changing hazards.

    A lightning strike within 10 miles triggers a stop-work alert and a 30-minute countdown. Each new nearby strike restarts it. River sites can also receive flood warnings after heavy overnight rain.

  3. 03

    Share what you see.

    Add a photo, pin the location, and describe a hazard for your team and future crews. An open high-severity report creates a warning until a supervisor resolves it.

  4. 04

    Focus attention where it matters.

    Supervisors compare sites on a map ranked by hazard score, inspect the factors behind each score, and review crew observations before allocating attention and resources.

  5. 05

    Prepare for the next shift.

    At 6 PM in each site’s local timezone, the daily briefing summarizes tomorrow’s hazards using the forecast and score. Open the briefing to review its date and the information it was based on.

THE HAZARD INDEX / 0–10

Two models. One explainable score.

The index combines a weather outlook for the next 24 hours with the likelihood of dangerous wildlife nearby this month. A higher number means greater predicted environmental risk.

Weather risk

0–8 points

A small decision-tree model—one that learns a sequence of yes/no checks—is trained on historical weather and NOAA Storm Events, including heat, lightning, flooding, wildfire, and high wind.

It uses the site’s next-24-hour forecast to estimate the chance of hazardous outdoor-work conditions.

Predicted probability × 8

Wildlife risk

0–2 points

A second decision tree learns from GBIF wildlife sightings, including iNaturalist records of alligators, cottonmouths, rattlesnakes, and copperheads.

It uses the site’s location and the current month to estimate the chance of dangerous wildlife nearby.

Predicted probability × 2

How do the individual factors add up?

The breakdown starts at a baseline, the model’s starting score. Each factor then raises or lowers it. In a waterfall chart, these changes lead from the baseline to the final score.

The same 8.2-point example, explained factor by factor

A negative contribution lowers the model’s estimate; it does not mean a hazard is impossible. These example values explain the model design and are not current site readings.

Alerts work independently. The score’s lightning factor is a forecast. An actual nearby strike triggers the stop-work rule regardless of the score.

Reports add context. Crew reports sit alongside the score and can trigger warnings. They do not change the trained score in this version.

Understand the limits. Models train on older years and are tested on the most recent year. Wildlife sightings are less complete in remote areas. The index is not an injury prediction; a rules-based fallback must be labeled as such.
